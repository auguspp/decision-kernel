## 6. Highest-priority next task — Sanhua robot operating economics

The next conversation should preferably start here.

The current robot success-state bridge depends on four sell-side variables:

```text
1m units
× CNY50k value content
× 70% share
× 10% net margin
```

We have labelled these correctly as assumptions, but have **not independently underwritten them**.

The next useful Research task is to decompose them into a real operating model.

### 6.1 Product / component architecture

Research:

- what actuator assemblies / subcomponents are actually supplied;
- which parts Sanhua manufactures internally;
- which parts are externally sourced;
- what “CNY50k per robot” includes and excludes;
- how value content may change as robot architecture matures.

### 6.2 Competitive structure

Research:

- serious competitors by component;
- qualification barriers;
- whether customers require second sourcing;
- customer bargaining power;
- localization / price-down pressure;
- whether current early share can survive mass production.

### 6.3 Share underwriting

Do not repeat “70% share” without asking:

```text
early design-in share
vs
qualified supplier share
vs
mass-production allocation share
vs
long-run economic share
```

The proof ladder matters.

### 6.4 Margin / manufacturing economics

Build the bridge:

```text
volume
× ASP / value content
→ revenue
→ gross margin
→ opex burden
→ operating / net profit
```

Research:

- yield;
- automation;
- utilization;
- price-down path;
- raw-material / purchased-content burden;
- customer concentration.

### 6.5 Capital intensity and incremental ROIC

This is currently the biggest missing layer in the success-state valuation.

Research:

```text
incremental revenue
→ incremental NOPAT / owner earnings
→ working capital
→ growth capex
→ development / R&D burden
→ incremental invested capital
→ incremental ROIC
→ owner cash
```

A 35亿元 profit state that requires CNY8bn of incremental capital is economically different from one that requires CNY30bn.

If capital allocation cannot be separated from company-wide expansion, say `NOT ESTABLISHED`.

### 6.6 Outside view / reference class

Build a small human-readable reference class, not a probability engine.

Question:

> Among industrial suppliers that reached `batch delivery / production-line ramp` in a new category, what happened over the next 3-5 years to volume, share, ASP, margin and ROIC?

Possible reference-class dimensions:

- high-value automotive / industrial component ramps;
- qualified supplier entering a new adjacent category;
- customer-concentrated early ramps;
- capacity built ahead of demand;
- high initial value content subject to price-down.

Record medians / ranges / failure modes where supportable.

Do not force a cardinal robot success probability from a tiny sample.
