## Human follow-up thought

After publication, the Human identified a sharper second-order question behind the original hook:

> The stock price had returned close to the level around the first InP-acquisition disclosure, which made the new InP business look almost "free". But before the InP acquisition, the company itself already had a market value of more than CNY4bn / roughly CNY5bn. Why was the legacy company worth that much then, and is that historical market value still a valid anchor today?

This is worth preserving because it exposes a hidden premise inside the intuitive subtraction:

```text
current market value
- pre-acquisition market value
= value assigned to InP
```

That subtraction only works if the legacy business's current economic value is still approximately equal to its historical pre-acquisition market value.

The Xingye Research already gives reasons to challenge that premise: legacy revenue has not collapsed, but profit, margin and capital efficiency have weakened; governance / investigation risk is also different from the pre-acquisition state. Therefore the historical pre-acquisition share price is useful as a market-history hook, but it is not evidence that today's legacy leather business still deserves the same value.

A better analytical question is:

> **How much of today's market value can the legacy leather economics still explain, and how much residual value must now be carried by InP, other growth expectations, or market-expression premium?**

This does not change the frozen Xingye Research or the already published note. It is a post-publication Research / writing prompt that may be useful if the case is revisited.

## Method / writing boundary

Do not generalize from this single case into a universal publishing rule.

The reusable caution is narrower:

- a historical price or market cap can be a powerful hook;
- but when it is used as a valuation anchor, the publication should check whether the underlying economic object is still comparable;
- `same price as before` does not imply `same legacy value as before`;
- a new-business "free option" argument can silently assume away deterioration in the old business.
