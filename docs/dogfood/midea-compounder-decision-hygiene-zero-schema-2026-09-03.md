## 0. Purpose

The current live dogfoods are dominated by cyclical, supply-demand, industrial and network cases:

- GigaDevice: cycle-amplified fabless memory platform;
- Micron: capital-intensive memory oligopoly / strategic-memory transition;
- YTO: franchised express network with uncertain harvest economics;
- Sanhua: qualified industrial core plus low-maturity optionality.

Midea is deliberately different.

The question is not primarily:

> Which one of several large discrete worlds will occur?

It is:

> **Can a mature, high-ROE franchise continue to convert a modest operating-growth rate, disciplined reinvestment, cash dividends and genuine per-share shrinkage into an attractive owner return at the price paid?**

This note tests whether Decision Hygiene should sometimes prefer a **per-share owner-return decomposition** over a forced cardinal scenario distribution.

It does not create:

- a Human investment decision;

- a cardinal probability distribution;
- production Numerical Odds;
- a Kernel schema or Constitution change;

## 5. Earnings-quality caution — do not over-read deducted NP

H1 2026 creates a useful accounting stress test.

RMB appreciation / FX movements can produce:

```text
FX losses -> recurring financial expense / operating classifications
hedging derivative gains -> partly non-recurring classification
```

Therefore:

```text
reported attributable NP
!= clean operating NP
!= deducted NP by itself
```

A rigorous owner-earnings bridge should separate:

1. core segment operating profit;
2. FX translation / transaction effects;
3. hedge economics that offset those exposures;
4. fair-value gains / losses on financial assets;
5. impairment and other one-offs;
6. finance-business cash-flow effects.

The lesson is methodological:

> **A mechanical preference for “扣非” can be as misleading as a mechanical preference for headline NP when the hedge and FX legs sit on different accounting lines.**

## 6. Capital allocation — gross buyback is not owner yield

This is the most important Midea-specific Decision Hygiene issue.

### 6.1 2025 dividends

The 2025 annual report shows total cash dividends of approximately:

```text
CNY32.36bn
```

The full-year dividend was `CNY4.3/share`, including the already-paid interim dividend and final dividend proposal / implementation process.

At the approximately `CNY86.98` Sep-2 public price context, the trailing `CNY4.3` cash dividend corresponds to roughly:

```text
4.3 / 86.98 ~= 4.9%
```

This is a real owner-cash-return component, subject to tax / timing differences by holder.

### 6.2 Gross buybacks are heterogeneous

The 2025 annual report records substantial cash used for repurchases, but **repurchase purpose matters**.

Midea has both:

- repurchases that are ultimately canceled and reduce share count;
- repurchases held for employee stock ownership / equity incentives.

Only the first category is automatically comparable to a direct per-share capital return.

Employee-plan repurchases can still be rational compensation / incentive design, but they should not be counted one-for-one as shareholder yield. They may merely offset future dilution or transfer value to employees.

### 6.3 2025 actual share cancellation is the cleaner owner-return signal

Midea completed material cancellation of repurchased shares during 2025, including a `95,000,000`-share cancellation late in the year plus an earlier `11,090,741`-share cancellation.

The 2025 report's dividend / repurchase disclosures indicate that canceled-repurchase economics were material.

For Decision Hygiene the preferred metric is therefore:

```text
cash dividend
+ cost of shares actually canceled / durable net share shrinkage
```

rather than:

```text
gross repurchase cash = shareholder yield
```

### 6.4 2026 repurchase program is mostly an incentive / employee-plan instrument

The March 30, 2026 A-share repurchase plan authorized approximately:

```text
CNY6.5bn to CNY13.0bn
price cap = CNY100/share
purpose = equity incentives and/or employee stock ownership plans
```

The plan can use internal funds and/or a dedicated stock-repurchase loan; the disclosed bank facility can fund up to a large proportion of actual repurchases within its terms.

That means:

> **A leverage-funded employee-plan buyback is not equivalent to a canceled-share buyback.**

It can be good capital allocation, neutral dilution management, or expensive compensation depending on future outcomes. The gross amount should not be embedded as a permanent owner yield.

## 7. The compounder return equation

For this economic species, the most useful first-pass underwriting is:

```text
per-share market return
≈ per-share owner-earnings growth
+ cash dividend yield
+ genuine net share shrinkage / per-share accretion
+/- valuation multiple change
```

The operating-growth component itself can be decomposed as:

```text
growth ≈ retained earnings ratio x incremental return on retained capital
```

This equation is intentionally approximate. It is a causal checklist, not an accounting identity.

### Why it matters

If Midea distributes roughly 70%+ of earnings as cash dividends, only roughly 30% or less remains for reinvestment before considering other capital-allocation flows.

To sustain, for example, a mid-single-digit organic per-share earnings growth rate with a high payout, retained capital must continue to earn a high incremental return.

Therefore:

> **The real compounder question is not whether historical ROE was ~20%. It is whether the next yuan retained in overseas OBM, building technology, industrial technology, robotics, logistics and other growth pools can still earn a return consistent with the desired per-share growth.**

## 11. Decision-critical variables

### 11.1 Smart Home moat / margin durability

Need to establish whether the mature cash engine can:

- maintain domestic competitive position without destructive pricing;
- preserve product / channel economics;
- support overseas brand-building rather than subsidize weak adjacencies;
- continue converting accounting profit to cash.

### 11.2 Overseas OBM economics

The relevant question is not overseas revenue growth by itself.

It is:

```text
overseas own-brand revenue
-> gross margin / operating margin
-> working capital
-> capex / marketing investment
-> incremental ROIC
```

Sell-side claims about OBM mix are useful expectation context, but company-level realized margin / return evidence should own Fundamental Belief.

### 11.3 B2B incremental ROIC

Building Technologies currently shows attractive growth / margin characteristics; Industrial Technology is materially weaker in the H1 snapshot; Robotics grows but still requires capital-return proof.

The research should therefore avoid a generic:

> “B2B grows faster, therefore the multiple should rise.”

Instead ask:

> **Which B2B pools can absorb retained capital at returns that preserve group-level per-share compounding?**

### 11.4 Cash payout versus retained-capital productivity

High payout is not a weakness if reinvestment opportunities are mature.

Lower payout is not automatically bullish if the retained capital earns mediocre returns.

The correct bridge is:

```text
retention
x incremental ROIC
-> sustainable per-share earnings growth
```

### 11.5 True net share shrinkage

Monitor:

- shares canceled;
- shares reissued / granted under incentives;
- treasury-share movements;
- diluted share count;
- cost of cancellation repurchases;
- whether employee-plan buybacks actually offset dilution or create it later.

The key metric is **net per-share accretion**, not gross repurchase cash.

### 11.6 Earnings-quality normalization

Reconcile headline NP, deducted NP, FX, hedge results, fair-value changes and finance-business effects before changing the long-run earnings base.

## 13. What would weaken or change the frame

Reopen skeptically if:

- Smart Home margin / share deteriorates structurally;
- overseas growth requires persistent low-return marketing / channel / manufacturing capital;
- B2B revenue grows but segment returns stay below the group's cost of capital or materially below the mature core;
- cash conversion weakens after normalizing finance-business flows;
- retained capital rises while incremental ROIC falls;
- gross buybacks remain large but diluted shares do not shrink because incentive issuance absorbs the repurchases;
- debt-funded or leverage-assisted repurchases become a material substitute for genuine owner cash generation;
- headline NP increasingly depends on financial-asset / fair-value / FX effects rather than operating economics.

A deeper negative frame change would be:

> **Midea is no longer a high-ROIC capital allocator with a mature cash engine, but a conglomerate using core cash flow to subsidize lower-return growth pools while reported buybacks obscure weak per-share accretion.**

Current evidence does not establish that negative frame; it is a falsifiable competitor.

## 14. Probability qualification

Probability knowledge is materially stronger here than in YTO or GigaDevice because:

- the economic frame is more stable;
- the mature core has a long realized operating history;
- earnings / cash distributions are less dominated by one commodity or supply-cycle variable;
- major decision variables are gradual and observable.

But this does **not** force a cardinal probability distribution.

Current best representation:

```text
CORE DURABILITY KNOWLEDGE = meaningful
RETURN-DECOMPOSITION KNOWLEDGE = meaningful
GLOBAL / B2B INCREMENTAL ROIC = partially established / heterogeneous
TRUE FUTURE NET SHARE SHRINKAGE = not fully established
EXACT TERMINAL MULTIPLE = market-dependent / not Fundamental probability
CARDINAL TOTAL-COMPANY WORLD DISTRIBUTION = not established
NUMERICAL ODDS = withheld
```

This case therefore supports a broader method hypothesis:

> **Probability maturity is economic-species dependent. Some compounders can be decision-useful before a cardinal world distribution exists because a large part of the underwriting is better expressed as continuous return decomposition.**

No Constitution change is authorized by one case.
