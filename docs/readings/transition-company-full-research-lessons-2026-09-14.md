## Why this note exists

The Xingye Technology dogfood exposed several connected Research failures that can recur whenever a listed company is shifting capital from a mature / deteriorating legacy business into an early new business.

The first Research pass was numerically consistent but structurally incomplete. It tended to model:

```text
legacy weak + new business weak
legacy normal + new business normal
legacy strong + new business strong
```

That omitted the most decision-relevant transition world:

```text
legacy economics deteriorate
+
new business genuinely succeeds and takes over value contribution
```

The correction is not “always add one more scenario.” It is to choose the correct Reference Frame and model the economically independent axes before combining them.

## 1. Do not assume a stable legacy base in a transition company

A company can have flat / rising revenue while economic value deteriorates.

Before calling a legacy business a valuation floor, inspect at least:

- normalized operating / parent profit trend;
- ROE / ROIC trend;
- gross-margin and mix changes;
- cash conversion;
- capex and working-capital needs;
- capacity growth versus utilization;
- whether new capital is earning acceptable incremental returns.

Rule:

> `LEGACY REVENUE STABLE` does not imply `LEGACY VALUE STABLE`.

A historical pre-acquisition share price is market history, not proof of the current legacy-business floor.

## 2. Model legacy and new business as separate causal axes first

For a material transformation case, build separate underwriting for:

```text
Axis A — legacy economics
Axis B — new-business commercialization
Axis C — capital allocation / financing where load-bearing
Axis D — governance / evidence-integrity state where load-bearing
```

Only then construct joint worlds.

At minimum, test anti-diagonal worlds when economically plausible:

- legacy deteriorates + new succeeds;
- legacy holds + new fails;
- legacy deteriorates + new fails;
- legacy stabilizes / repairs + new succeeds.

Do not use only low-low / mid-mid / high-high worlds. A clean diagonal table can hide the actual investment transition.

## 3. Acquisition reality, exclusivity and economic success are different claims

Preserve the proof ladder:

```text
signed transaction
!= asset / team / IP transferred
!= exclusive capability
!= customer certification
!= repeat orders
!= scale production
!= durable margin
!= owner cash
!= high-ROIC growth
```

A company can buy real assets and still fail economically. A seller can transfer a real asset group while retaining related capability elsewhere. Non-compete geography, scope and duration must be read exactly rather than upgraded into “bought the whole business” or “national exclusivity.”

## 4. New-business proof ladder must prevent capability-to-profit jumps

For an early industrial business, use a staged ladder such as:

```text
project / acquisition intent
→ physical assets, team and IP transfer
→ customer qualification
→ small-batch delivery
→ repeat purchase
→ material orders
→ stable capacity ramp
→ yield / ASP / gross margin visibility
→ cash collection
→ acceptable post-expansion ROIC
```

Industry demand, TAM, customer certification and installed capacity are not substitutes for later rungs.

If current evidence stops at certification / small batch, future billion-yuan revenue may be used as a success-state challenger when industry scale supports it, but not as Base, expected earnings or probability-weighted value.

## 5. Use peers for scale and proof patterns, not probability transfer

Peer evidence can answer:

- What order size exists in the industry?
- What capacity scale is real?
- What mature commercialization evidence looks like?
- What capital / customer commitments often accompany scale-up?

It cannot automatically answer:

- whether the target company will win those orders;
- whether its yield, ASP, margin or customer terms match;
- how likely its success is.

Rule:

> `PEER SUCCESS SCALE` is a feasibility / outside-view anchor, not a success probability for the researched company.

## 6. Terminal P/E must be underwritten independently from terminal profit

Do not let the same success story silently raise both earnings and the terminal multiple.

For a higher terminal P/E, separately support why the company at the horizon has:

- longer remaining growth duration;
- attractive reinvestment returns;
- durable competitive advantage / customer stickiness;
- better cash conversion;
- lower business / governance risk;
- a market expression premium, if explicitly separated from Fundamental Belief.

Required challenge:

```text
new business succeeds
→ profit rises
```

and independently:

```text
new business succeeds in a way that lengthens duration / improves ROIC / lowers risk
→ justified multiple may rise
```

If the second bridge is absent, do not pair the highest profit with the highest P/E by default.

## 7. Similar price outputs from different models do not validate the old model

In the Xingye case, an early model and the corrected transition model both produced numbers around the low-teens under some conditions, but for completely different reasons.

Rule:

> Numerical coincidence is not model validation.

Whenever the Reference Frame or causal bridge changes, preserve an explicit old→new revision bridge:

- old model;
- challenged assumption;
- new model;
- why it explains the case better;
- whether old calculations remain only conditional historical arithmetic;
- what future evidence discriminates the models.

Do not silently keep an old entry price because the corrected model happens to land nearby.

## 8. Governance / regulatory investigation is not a generic valuation haircut

If a company is under investigation and the exact scope is unknown, avoid invented `-10%`, `-20%` or arbitrary P/E penalties.

Separate:

```text
limited disclosure / process issue
→ may raise required return / validation burden
```

from:

```text
investigation challenges the transaction, customer, financial or other load-bearing evidence
→ evidence lineage is challenged; Research must reopen
```

If the investigation attacks the source claims supporting the thesis, this is not merely an Odds haircut.

## 9. Capital migration must be modeled, not treated as free transformation

A legacy company entering a capital-intensive new business can destroy value even when the new product sells.

Research must ask:

- how much legacy capex continues while the legacy business weakens;
- how much new-business capex / working capital is required;
- whether debt, equity issuance or dilution is needed;
- whether early new-business accounting profit is offset by ramp capital;
- when and whether the new business becomes self-funding;
- whether consolidated owner cash improves before declaring the transformation successful.

A successful product is not necessarily a successful capital-allocation outcome.

## 10. Current price should reveal what transition success is already being paid for

For a transformation stock, reverse current price into explicit requirements under multiple legacy states.

Preferred chain:

```text
observed / context price
→ required horizon equity value
→ plausible legacy value at that horizon
→ residual required from new business
→ new-business profit / cash required under bounded multiples
→ revenue / capacity / margin / capital requirements where supportable
→ compare with current proof ladder
```

This prevents phrases such as “new business is free” merely because price returned to a historical pre-acquisition level.

## 11. First Entry is not the most conservative world clearing the hurdle

Do not accidentally turn First Entry into HIGH-MOS by requiring the central business world to clear the hurdle even under a deliberately depressed valuation multiple.

For each candidate price, state:

- which non-extreme worlds clear the return hurdle;
- which worlds still lose money and by how much;
- which unverified expectations remain paid for;
- whether the candidate requires a right-tail world;
- what evidence would invalidate the price card.

A FIRST-ENTRY REVIEW can still contain material downside. It is not a certified floor.

Conversely, if only a strong right-tail world works, label the price `RE-UNDERWRITE` / `SUCCESS-DEPENDENT`, not First Entry merely to produce a usable number.

## 12. Transition-company minimum review before decision-use First Entry

Before treating a first-entry boundary as method-ready in a material business transformation, the reviewer should be able to answer:

1. What is the legacy business's economic-value direction, not just revenue direction?
2. What capital is still being consumed by the legacy business?
3. What exact assets / team / IP / rights were acquired, and what was not acquired?
4. What rung of the new-business proof ladder is actually established?
5. What anti-diagonal worlds have been tested?
6. What new-business scale must be reached for the current price or candidate entry to work?
7. What capex / working capital / financing is required to reach that scale?
8. Why does the terminal multiple follow from post-horizon duration / ROIC rather than from success-label enthusiasm?
9. Could a regulatory / governance event challenge the evidence itself rather than only valuation?
10. Is the proposed first entry robust to at least one plausible transition world that does not require the strongest right tail?

If these cannot be reasonably bounded, output:

```text
BOUNDED CONDITIONAL REQUIREMENTS
FIRST ENTRY = NOT METHOD-READY
```

rather than inventing precision.

## 13. What this note does not do

This note does not create:

- a new Kernel schema;
- a transition-company score;
- mandatory Monte Carlo / probability machinery;
- a generic fixed P/E table;
- a new Research workflow;
- investment authority.

It is a reusable Research discipline earned by a real failure. Requirements definition / prioritization and runtime implementation remain in their existing owners.
